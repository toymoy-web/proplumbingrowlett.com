Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation in Rowlett

A garbage disposal that hums but will not spin, leaks from underneath, or has simply gone silent is one of the most common kitchen calls in Rowlett. Rowlett Pro Plumbing repairs and replaces disposals same day in most cases.

Common Problems

  • ✓Humming sound but the blades will not turn, usually a jam that needs a reset.
  • ✓Leaking from underneath, often the sink flange seal or a cracked housing.
  • ✓No sound at all, could be a tripped reset button, a bad switch, or a dead unit.
  • ✓Frequent jamming even after clearing it, a sign the unit itself is wearing out.

Clearing A Minor Jam Safely

Before You Reach In

  • ✓Turn the disposal off at the wall switch, then also flip the breaker for that circuit, never rely on the switch alone.
  • ✓Use a hex wrench in the bottom center hole, if your unit has one, to manually rotate the blades and free the jam.
  • ✓Never put a hand inside the disposal, even with the power off, use tongs or the hex wrench.
  • ✓If the reset button on the bottom of the unit trips repeatedly after clearing a jam, that points to a motor issue worth a professional look.

How Long A Disposal Actually Lasts

A quality disposal under normal household use typically lasts 8 to 12 years before the motor or seals wear out. A unit that jams repeatedly well before that age is usually a sign of either consistent overuse, fibrous scraps that should go in the trash instead, or a unit that was undersized for the household's actual needs from the start.

Choosing A Replacement Disposal

Horsepower is the main real decision when replacing a disposal. A 1/3 horsepower unit handles light, occasional use fine for a smaller household. A 1/2 to 3/4 horsepower unit runs quieter and jams less often under heavier daily use, and it is usually worth the modest price difference if the old unit was jamming frequently rather than simply failing from age.

Reconnecting the dishwasher drain line correctly during a swap matters too, a disposal installed without knocking out the dishwasher inlet plug is a real, avoidable mistake that causes a dishwasher backup the first time it runs.

Codes This Work Follows

Disposal installation follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and International Plumbing Code (IPC) the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners adopts statewide, including correct drain trap sizing and dishwasher connection requirements.

Common Questions

My disposal hums but won't spin, is that fixable?+

Usually yes. A humming disposal with no spin almost always means the blades are physically jammed, and clearing the jam and resetting the internal breaker button on the bottom of the unit resolves it in most cases without needing a new disposal.

What should never go into a garbage disposal?+

Grease, fibrous vegetable peels like celery, coffee grounds in large amounts, and bones all cause repeat jams and premature wear. None of these damage a disposal instantly, but they are the most common reason a unit fails years earlier than it should.

Why does my disposal smell bad even when it seems to work fine?+

A smell without a jam usually means small food particles are trapped on the underside of the splash guard or in the grind chamber where daily use does not fully rinse them out. Running the disposal with cold water and a few ice cubes helps scrub the chamber, but a persistent smell after that is worth checking in person, since it can also point to a slow drain trap behind it.

Is it normal for a disposal to leak a little when it's brand new?+

No, a new disposal should not leak at all. A small leak right after installation usually means a mounting bolt was not tightened evenly around the sink flange, or the plumber's putty seal under the flange did not seat fully. It is worth a quick follow-up rather than assuming it will seal itself over time.

Can I install a garbage disposal myself?+

It is possible for someone comfortable with basic electrical and plumbing work, but the two most common DIY mistakes, forgetting to knock out the dishwasher inlet plug and an uneven flange seal, both cause real problems later. A professional install takes under an hour in most kitchens and avoids both.
