Tankless Water Heater Service in Rowlett

A tankless water heater heats water on demand instead of storing it, which means no more running out mid-shower, and a smaller footprint than a traditional tank. Rowlett Pro Plumbing installs, services, and repairs tankless units across Rowlett.

Hard Water And Tankless Units

Tankless units are actually more sensitive to hard water mineral scale than a standard tank, since the heat exchanger's narrow passages clog with scale faster than a tank's larger storage volume does. That makes the annual flush schedule genuinely more important on a tankless unit in this area's water than it would be in a genuinely soft-water market, not just a generic maintenance suggestion.

Is Tankless Right For Your Home

  • ✓You regularly run out of hot water with a traditional tank.
  • ✓You want to free up closet or garage space currently taken by a tank.
  • ✓Your home already has, or can support, the gas line sizing or electrical capacity a tankless unit needs.
  • ✓You plan to stay in the home long enough to see the return on the higher upfront cost.

Common Service Calls

Most tankless service calls are not a full failure, they are a unit throwing an error code, short-cycling, or delivering water that is not as hot as it used to be. Mineral scale buildup inside the heat exchanger, especially without a regular flush schedule, is the leading cause of all three.

A yearly descaling flush is the single most effective thing an owner can do to extend a tankless unit's life, and it is a quick, inexpensive visit compared to the cost of replacing a heat exchanger that scaled up from neglect.

Electric Versus Gas Tankless

An electric tankless unit installs more easily since it does not need gas venting, but it typically requires a significant electrical panel upgrade to supply enough amperage for whole-house demand, which is not always a small or cheap addition. A gas tankless unit usually delivers higher flow rates for whole-house use and is the more common choice for a full-size home, provided the gas line and venting can support it.

Sizing A Tankless Unit Correctly

Undersizing is the most common mistake in a tankless conversion. A unit is rated by how many gallons per minute it can heat at a given temperature rise, and that rating has to cover your household's actual peak simultaneous use, not just its average use. Two showers running at once during a busy morning is a realistic peak for many households, and a unit sized only for one shower will genuinely disappoint no matter how well it is installed.

This is worked out during the quote by reviewing what actually runs at the same time in your home, not by picking a generic model off a shelf.

Codes This Work Follows

Tankless installation follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and International Plumbing Code (IPC) the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners adopts statewide, including required venting clearances and gas line sizing verification.

Common Questions

How often does a tankless water heater need to be flushed?+

Once a year for most households, more often on genuinely hard water. Skipping the flush is the single biggest reason a tankless unit's performance drops or the heat exchanger fails years earlier than it should.

Will a tankless unit work with my existing gas line?+

Not always without modification. Tankless units generally require a higher gas flow rate than a standard tank, so the existing gas line size and meter capacity need to be checked before installation, which we do as part of any tankless quote, not as a surprise add-on afterward.

Is it true tankless units never run out of hot water?+

Practically, yes, within the unit's flow rate limit. A single tankless unit can still be outpaced if too many fixtures run simultaneously, a shower and a dishwasher and a washing machine all at once. That is why sizing the unit to the home's actual peak demand matters during installation, not as an afterthought left for later.
