A Broken Valve Or Supply Line Under The Sink

A dripping faucet can wait. A cracked supply line or a stop valve that has snapped off in your hand cannot, because water keeps pouring under the cabinet until it is closed.

Our 24/7 line covers these failures. We replace the failed valve or line, test the fixture and confirm the price before we begin.

Repair Or Replace

Most faucet drips are a worn internal cartridge, and on a name-brand faucet that is usually a straightforward part swap rather than a full replacement. A faucet from a builder-grade, no-name brand, or one that is already 15-plus years old often does not have replacement cartridges readily available, and full replacement ends up being the more practical fix.

An under-sink leak at a supply line or shutoff valve is almost always a fitting or valve replacement rather than anything to do with the faucet itself, and it is usually a quick, inexpensive fix once the actual connection is identified.

Hard Water And Faucet Wear

North Texas water is moderately hard, and mineral scale builds up inside a faucet's internal cartridge and around the aerator screen faster here than it would on genuinely soft water. That buildup is a common reason a faucet starts feeling stiff, drips even after a repair, or loses pressure at just that one fixture while the rest of the house is fine.

A quick aerator cleaning is worth trying before assuming a bigger problem: unscrew the aerator at the tip of the faucet, soak it in vinegar for a few minutes, and rinse. If pressure improves, that was the whole issue. If it does not, the cartridge or the supply line itself is the more likely cause.

Kitchen Versus Bathroom Faucets

A kitchen faucet works harder than a bathroom faucet, more daily cycles, more temperature swings, and often a pull-down sprayer hose with its own wear points. That is why a kitchen faucet's internal parts tend to wear out first even on a newer home, while a bathroom faucet in the same house can go years longer before needing attention.

Codes This Work Follows

Supply line and fixture work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and International Plumbing Code (IPC) the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners adopts statewide, including correct backflow prevention at fixture connections.

Faucets In Waterview-Era Kitchens And Baths

Fixtures installed when Waterview was built, in the late 1990s and early 2000s, are now 25 years old, and cartridges, O-rings, and supply connectors wear out on that schedule. A drip is usually a worn cartridge and costs far less to fix than the water it wastes. If the faucet body itself is corroded, replacement is the honest answer.

Outdoor Spigots, Shower Valves And Shutoffs

Not every faucet call is a kitchen sink. Outdoor spigots drip at the stem or split after a freeze, shower and tub valves leak behind the wall, and laundry sinks have their own supply valves.

North Texas does get hard freezes, so an exposed spigot is worth protecting before winter, and one that split in cold weather needs replacing as soon as it thaws.

Old stop valves under sinks often seize or drip at the stem. Replacing a worn stop at the same visit means the next repair or fixture swap does not turn into a main-shutoff job.

Common Questions

Is emergency faucet and sink repair available in Rowlett?+

Yes. A spraying supply line or a failed stop valve is handled 24/7, and the first step is stopping the water.

We replace the broken part, test every connection on the fixture and leave the shutoffs working so the next repair is not a main-shutoff job.

Why does my faucet still drip after I replaced the washer myself?+

A drip that continues after a washer swap usually means the actual worn part is the cartridge or the valve seat, not the washer, or the replacement part was slightly the wrong size for that specific faucet model. A technician can confirm the exact part needed rather than guessing through a second DIY attempt.

Can you match my existing bathroom or kitchen faucet finish?+

In most cases yes, for common finishes like chrome, brushed nickel, and matte black. For a discontinued or unusual finish, we will let you know upfront if an exact match is not realistic so you can decide before we start.

Is it worth repairing an old faucet or just replacing it?+

If the faucet is a recognizable name brand and the body itself is in good shape, a cartridge swap is almost always cheaper and faster than a full replacement. Once a faucet is old enough that the manufacturer no longer stocks parts for it, or the finish has worn through in spots, replacement usually makes more sense than chasing down an obsolete part.

Can a single-handle faucet be repaired the same way as a two-handle one?+

The repair concept is the same, isolate the water, replace the worn internal part, but the parts themselves differ. A single-handle faucet usually has one cartridge controlling both hot and cold, while a two-handle faucet has a separate stem or cartridge for each side, which can mean one side wears out well before the other.

Do you carry parts for older or discontinued faucet models?+

We carry the common current cartridges and stems for major brands, and can usually source an older or less common part ahead of the visit once you tell us the brand and rough age of the faucet on the phone.

Can you replace a leaking shower valve or tub faucet?+

Yes. A dripping showerhead is often a worn cartridge, but a leak behind the wall may mean opening the wall to reach the valve. We explain which one it is before starting.
