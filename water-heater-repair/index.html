Water Heaters

Water Heater Repair in Rowlett

A water heater rarely fails with warning. One day the shower runs cold, or you notice a puddle spreading across the garage floor near the tank, and now it is a same-day problem. Rowlett Pro Plumbing repairs both gas and electric water heaters across Rowlett, TX, and diagnoses the actual failure before recommending a fix.

Signs Your Water Heater Needs A Repair Call

  • ✓No hot water, or hot water that runs out much faster than it used to.
  • ✓A puddle or damp spot at the base of the tank, especially near the garage slab edge.
  • ✓Rust-colored water, a rotten-egg smell, or a rumbling or popping noise from the tank.
  • ✓A pilot light that will not stay lit on a gas unit, or a tripped breaker on an electric one.

What Usually Causes It

Most tank failures in this area trace back to sediment buildup from hard water settling at the bottom of the tank, which insulates the burner or lower element and forces it to work harder until it fails outright. A leaking tank is different: once the steel liner corrodes through, the tank itself cannot be patched or resealed, and replacement is the only real fix.

A leak at the top of the tank, around a fitting or the cold-water inlet, is usually a loose connection or a failed T&P relief valve, and that is genuinely repairable without replacing the whole unit. Telling the two apart before quoting a price is the first thing a technician checks on site.

Tank Or Tankless

Most Rowlett homes still run a standard tank water heater, gas or electric, and repair on these is usually the fastest and cheapest fix when the problem is a thermostat, element, pilot assembly, or gas control valve rather than the tank itself. Tankless units are becoming more common in newer builds and remodels because they take up less space and do not run out of hot water, but they fail differently. A tankless unit that starts short-cycling or throwing an error code is almost always a mineral scale or flow-sensor issue rather than a burned-out heating element. It needs a technician who actually works on tankless units regularly, not just tank heaters.

Hard water is common across the Dallas-Fort Worth area, and mineral buildup inside a tankless heat exchanger, or settled sediment at the bottom of a tank, is the single most preventable cause of an early failure on either type. A yearly flush adds real years to either unit's service life.

Before You Call: Quick Safety Checks

  • ✓For a gas unit with no hot water, check whether the pilot light is lit before assuming the worst.
  • ✓For an electric unit, check the breaker panel for a tripped breaker first.
  • ✓If you smell gas anywhere near the unit, leave the house, call your gas utility, and do not attempt to relight anything yourself.
  • ✓If the tank itself is visibly leaking from the bottom, shut off the cold water supply valve at the top of the tank to limit water damage.

Common Questions

Can a leaking water heater actually be repaired?+

It depends on where the leak is. A leak at a fitting, valve, or connection on top of the tank is usually repairable. A leak coming from the tank body itself means the steel liner has corroded through, and no amount of resealing fixes that, so replacement is the honest recommendation.

How long does a typical water heater last in this area?+

Most tank water heaters in Rowlett last 8 to 12 years, on the shorter end of that range for homes on harder water where sediment builds up faster. A tank pushing 12 years that starts acting up is usually closer to replacement than repair.
