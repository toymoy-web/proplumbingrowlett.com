Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement in Rowlett

Repiping means replacing the supply lines inside a home, the pipes running through walls and under floors that carry water to every fixture, not the service line running from the street to the house. Rowlett Pro Plumbing repipes homes across Rowlett when the original plumbing has reached the point where individual repairs no longer make sense.

Signs A Home Needs Repiping

  • ✓Multiple pipe leaks over the past year or two, not just one isolated incident.
  • ✓Discolored or rusty water, especially when a faucet has not been used in a while.
  • ✓Noticeably low water pressure across multiple fixtures at once.
  • ✓Visible corrosion or pinhole leaks on exposed galvanized or copper pipe.

Had more than one pipe leak this year? That pattern usually means repiping, not another patch.

Repiping Versus A Water Line Replacement

These are two different jobs that get confused often. Repiping replaces the supply lines inside the house, the network running through walls and under floors to every sink, shower, and appliance. A water line replacement, covered on its own page, replaces the single service line running underground from the street or meter to the house. A home can need one, the other, or occasionally both, and a proper inspection tells you which.

Why Older Rowlett Homes Come Up In This Conversation

A house in Rowlett built before the mid-1990s is more likely to still carry original copper or, in some cases, galvanized supply lines, and repeated small leaks in different rooms over a year or two is the pattern that usually points toward the whole system aging out at once rather than one unlucky section. That is different from a single isolated leak, which usually just needs a spot repair rather than a full repipe.

How A Repipe Is Done With Minimal Wall Damage

1

Plan The Route

New lines are routed to minimize the number of wall or ceiling openings needed.

2

Open Access Points

Small, targeted openings are cut only where necessary to run new pipe.

3

Run New Supply Lines

New PEX or copper lines are run to every fixture, old lines are capped or removed.

4

Patch And Pressure Test

Openings are left ready for drywall repair, and the whole system is pressure-tested before we consider the job done.

Codes This Work Follows

Repiping follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and International Plumbing Code (IPC) the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners adopts statewide, including correct pipe sizing, support spacing, and material approval for the application.

Common Questions

Do I have to move out of my house during a repipe?+

Not for most residential repipes. Water is shut off in sections as work moves through the house, and most homeowners stay in the home throughout, though water access to specific bathrooms or the kitchen may be limited for a few hours at a time.

Will repiping fix my low water pressure?+

If the low pressure is caused by corroded or narrowed old pipe, yes, new full-size lines typically restore pressure to normal. If the cause is something else, like a pressure-reducing valve or the municipal supply itself, we identify that during the assessment rather than assuming repiping is the fix.

How long does a whole-home repipe take?+

Most single-story homes are completed in 2 to 4 days. Larger or multi-story homes, or ones with difficult access, can take longer, and we give a realistic timeline as part of the quote rather than a generic estimate.

How much wall or ceiling repair will I need after a repipe?+

We plan the route to keep openings as few and as targeted as possible, but drywall or ceiling patching after a repipe is normal and expected. We leave every opening ready for a painter or drywall contractor, and can coordinate that follow-up work if you would rather not arrange it separately.

Can a repipe be done in phases instead of all at once?+

In some cases yes, particularly on a larger home where budget or scheduling makes a single continuous project difficult. Phasing means living with a mix of old and new lines for a period, which is not ideal long-term, but it is a real option worth discussing if a full repipe all at once is not currently feasible.
