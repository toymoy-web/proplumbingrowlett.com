Lake Ray Hubbard Drainage And Sump Pumps

A number of Rowlett properties near the lake or in low-lying yards run a sump pump to manage groundwater and storm runoff. A failed sump pump during a heavy North Texas storm is one of the more common calls we get from homes in Waterview and the lakeside sections of town. If your pump has not been tested in the last year, running it manually with a bucket of water poured into the pit is a quick way to confirm it still kicks on before the next storm does it for you.

Common Questions

Is a Rowlett plumber required to be licensed in Texas?+

Yes. Texas regulates plumbing statewide through the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners (TSBPE), not city by city. Every plumber working on a home in Rowlett needs a valid tradesman, journeyman, or master plumber license issued by that board, and any unlicensed work on gas lines, water lines, or drainage is a real code violation, not just a formality.

Do you offer free estimates?+

Yes, for non-emergency work we give a free on-site estimate before you commit to anything. For emergency dispatch, the diagnostic fee is disclosed upfront and is waived against the repair once you approve the work.

Why do so many Rowlett homes get slab leaks?+

Rowlett sits on expansive clay soil that swells when it is wet and shrinks when it dries out, and that constant movement puts real stress on the copper and cast-iron supply lines that run under a slab foundation. Add decades of wet-dry cycles around Lake Ray Hubbard and older copper lines in particular start to pit and leak from the outside in, well before the pipe itself is actually worn out.
