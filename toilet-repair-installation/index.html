Toilet Repair & Installation in Rowlett

A running toilet is one of the most common calls in Rowlett, and it is also one of the most expensive to ignore, a toilet that runs constantly can waste hundreds of gallons a day and show up as a real jump on the water bill. Rowlett Pro Plumbing handles everything from a simple fill valve swap to a full toilet replacement.

What We Fix

  • ✓Running toilet, usually a worn flapper or fill valve.
  • ✓Weak or incomplete flush, often a clogged rim or a flapper closing too early.
  • ✓Toilet rocking or leaking at the base, usually a failed wax ring.
  • ✓Constant refilling sound, cracked tank, or a toilet that is simply original to a much older home.

Toilet running nonstop? That is a fast, inexpensive fix most of the time.

Why Toilets Fail Faster In Some Rowlett Homes

A toilet bolted to a slab foundation in an older part of Rowlett can develop a rocking base over time as the slab itself shifts slightly with the area's clay soil, and a rocking toilet puts real, repeated stress on the wax ring seal underneath it. That is a different root cause from ordinary wear, and it is worth mentioning if your toilet keeps loosening again shortly after a repair.

Hard water across the Dallas-Fort Worth area also plays a role inside the tank itself, mineral buildup on a fill valve or flapper seat is one of the more common reasons a toilet that was recently repaired starts running again within a year or two.

What To Check Before You Call

  • ✓Lift the tank lid and check whether the flapper is sitting flush and sealing completely.
  • ✓Check whether the fill tube is aimed into the overflow tube rather than just near it.
  • ✓Feel the base of the toilet for movement while someone flushes, a rocking base is a real clue.
  • ✓Note whether the issue is constant running, a weak flush, or a base leak, that detail helps us bring the right parts on the first visit.

Water-Saving Upgrades Worth Considering

A pre-1994 toilet can use 3.5 to 5 gallons per flush, compared to 1.28 to 1.6 gallons for a current standard unit. On a home with an original toilet in every bathroom, replacing them is one of the few plumbing upgrades that pays for itself directly through a lower water bill, separate from any repair need.

Codes This Work Follows

Toilet installation and repair follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and International Plumbing Code (IPC) the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners adopts statewide, including correct rough-in clearance and flush-rating requirements for a code-compliant installation.

Common Questions

How much water does a running toilet actually waste?+

A toilet that runs continuously can waste anywhere from 200 to over 1,000 gallons a day depending on how the flapper or fill valve is failing, which is often the single biggest hidden line item on a water bill once it is fixed.

Can you match my existing toilet's water usage or bowl height?+

In most cases yes. We carry standard-height and comfort-height options in the common flush ratings, and we will confirm rough-in measurements before installing a replacement so it fits your existing space without surprises.

Why does my new toilet still run occasionally after installation?+

A newly installed toilet that runs intermittently, rather than constantly, usually means the flapper chain has too much slack or is caught under the flapper itself, a quick adjustment rather than a defective part. If it persists after checking the chain, the fill valve height may need a small readjustment to stop water from trickling into the overflow tube.

How long does a full toilet replacement actually take?+

A straightforward swap of an existing toilet for a new one on the same footprint usually takes under an hour once the new unit is on site. It runs longer only when the wax ring area or flange underneath needs repair first, which is exactly why we check that during the initial assessment rather than assuming a clean swap.
