A Quick Test You Can Run Yourself

Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit until the float rises. A working pump should kick on within a few seconds and clear the water. If it does not activate, activates but does not clear the pit, or runs but sounds labored, that is worth a service call before the next real storm tests it for you.

Choosing The Right Pump

A 1/3 horsepower pump handles most residential sump pits fine under normal conditions. But a home with a documented history of heavy water intake, or a lower elevation relative to Lake Ray Hubbard, is often better served by a 1/2 horsepower unit with a higher gallons-per-hour rating. Undersizing a pump is a common, avoidable reason a system that seems to be working still lets water in during a genuinely heavy storm.

A backup pump, either battery-powered or water-powered, adds real protection against the two failure modes a single pump cannot cover on its own: a power outage during the storm, and the primary pump failing mechanically at the worst possible moment.

Discharge Line Placement Matters

Where a sump pump's discharge line actually routes water matters as much as the pump itself. A discharge line that dumps water right back next to the foundation, rather than well away from the house, simply recycles the same water back into the soil the pump was meant to remove.

On a Rowlett property near the lake, routing the discharge toward an existing storm drain or a genuinely lower point of the yard, well clear of a neighboring property line, is part of doing the job right the first time.

Codes This Work Follows

Sump pump discharge routing follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and International Plumbing Code (IPC) the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners adopts statewide, including correct discharge location so water is not routed back toward the foundation or a neighboring property.

Drainage Pumps Near The Lake And Cottonwood Creek

Rowlett homes rarely have basements, so sump pumps here usually serve crawl spaces, yard drainage basins, and low spots near Lake Ray Hubbard, Lake Shore Village, and the Trails at Cottonwood Creek. The repair signs are specific. A pump that hums but does not move water usually has a failed impeller or a stuck float, while one that runs constantly often has a check valve problem. Test yours before the next storm, not during it.

Sump Pumps Versus Sewage Ejector Pumps

A sump pump moves groundwater or storm water away from a pit, while a sewage ejector lifts wastewater from a low bathroom or laundry to the sewer line. They look similar from above, but the ejector sits in a sealed basin and handles solids.

Failure signs differ too. A sump that cycles constantly or runs without moving water is failing, and an ejector that gurgles, alarms or lets a toilet drain slowly needs attention soon.

Common Questions

Is emergency sump pump repair available in Rowlett during a storm?+

Yes. Storm calls are the main reason this line exists, and a failed pump is treated as urgent.

A technician tests the pump, float and check valve, replaces what failed and discusses a battery backup so a power outage does not repeat the problem.

Should I get a battery backup for my sump pump?+

If your home is in a lower-lying area near Lake Ray Hubbard, yes, it is worth serious consideration. A power outage during a major storm is exactly when a sump pump is needed most, and a battery backup keeps it running through that outage instead of failing right when the rain is heaviest.

How often should a sump pump be tested?+

At minimum once at the start of storm season, and ideally every few months. The bucket test described above takes a few minutes and catches most failures before they matter.

What is the actual difference between a pedestal and a submersible pump?+

A pedestal pump keeps the motor above the pit, out of the water, which makes it easier to service but generally louder and less powerful. A submersible pump sits inside the pit itself, sealed against water, and is the quieter, higher-capacity option most homes with a real flooding risk are better served by.

Does the sump pump need to run on its own dedicated electrical circuit?+

Yes, that is the standard and safer setup. Sharing a circuit with other household loads raises the odds of a tripped breaker cutting power to the pump at exactly the moment it is needed most, during a storm when other appliances are also drawing power.

Do you replace sewage ejector pumps?+

Yes. If you have a below-grade bathroom or laundry on an ejector, tell the dispatcher when you call so the right pump is on the truck.

Do not wait for the next storm to find out your pump failed.
