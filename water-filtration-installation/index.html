Water Filtration Installation in Rowlett

Filtration and softening solve different problems, a softener addresses hardness minerals, while filtration targets taste, odor, chlorine, and sediment. Rowlett Pro Plumbing installs both whole-home and under-sink filtration systems across Rowlett, and can combine filtration with a softener when a household wants both.

Signs Filtration Might Help

  • ✓Tap water has a noticeable chlorine smell or taste, especially right after it comes out of the faucet.
  • ✓You currently buy bottled water regularly just to avoid the taste of tap water.
  • ✓Ice cubes or water from the fridge dispenser taste off compared to filtered alternatives.
  • ✓You want a real, independent step beyond municipal treatment alone for drinking water quality.

Reverse Osmosis Waste Water, A Real Trade-Off

A reverse osmosis system produces some wastewater as part of the filtration process, typically a few gallons for every gallon of filtered water produced with an older system, though newer, more efficient models have narrowed that ratio considerably. It is a genuine trade-off worth knowing about upfront rather than discovering after installation, and we explain the specific ratio for the unit we are proposing before you commit to it.

Maintenance Is What Makes The Investment Worth It

A filtration system that never gets its filters changed eventually performs worse than no filter at all, since a saturated carbon filter can actually harbor bacteria growth rather than continuing to improve water quality. We set up new installs with a clear maintenance schedule and can handle filter changes as a standing service if you would rather not track it yourself.

What Filtration Actually Improves

Municipal tap water in this area is treated and safe to drink, but many households still notice a chlorine taste or smell, which a whole-home carbon filter reduces noticeably at every fixture. A reverse osmosis system under the kitchen sink goes further for drinking and cooking water specifically, removing a broader range of dissolved substances than a carbon filter alone.

Filtration and a water softener are not competing systems, a softener removes hardness minerals that cause scale, while filtration addresses taste, odor, and sediment. Many households in this area run both together for the combined benefit.

Common Questions

Do I need both a softener and a filtration system?+

Not necessarily, it depends on what you are trying to fix. If scale and hard-water buildup are the main concern, a softener alone addresses that. If taste, odor, or drinking water quality specifically is the concern, filtration alone may be enough. Many households eventually add both once they experience the combined benefit.

How often do filtration system filters need to be replaced?+

It varies by system and household usage, typically every 6 to 12 months for a whole-home carbon filter and annually for reverse osmosis membranes and pre-filters, though heavier usage shortens that interval and hard water tends to clog a sediment pre-filter faster than expected.

Will a filtration system reduce my water pressure?+

A correctly sized system should not cause a noticeable pressure drop. An undersized whole-home filter, or one overdue for a filter change, is the usual cause when pressure does drop, which is why correct sizing at installation matters as much as the filtration technology itself.
