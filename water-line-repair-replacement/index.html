No Water, Or Water Pooling In The Yard

When the whole house loses water, or a soft wet patch appears between the meter and the front wall, the main supply line may have broken. The water keeps running into the ground until someone shuts it off.

Our 24/7 line sends a technician to locate the break, and you get the repair and replacement options priced before any digging.

Water Line Versus Repiping, The Real Difference

A water line problem sits outside the house, between the meter or curb stop and the point where the pipe enters the foundation. Repiping, covered on its own page, replaces the network of pipes inside the house after that entry point. A single failed water line does not mean the interior plumbing needs replacing, and interior repiping does not fix a failing service line, they are genuinely separate repairs even though both eventually deliver the same water.

What Actually Causes A Water Line To Fail

Root intrusion from a mature tree near the line's path, corrosion on an old galvanized service line, and ground shifting from Rowlett's clay soil are the three most common causes we find once a line is actually opened up. A locate and camera pass before digging confirms which of these applies rather than guessing based on the home's age alone.

Locating The Line Before Any Digging

Before any excavation starts, we locate the exact path of the existing line and mark other utilities in the area, a step that matters as much for avoiding an accidental gas or electric line strike as it does for finding the water line itself. A proper locate also confirms the real depth and material of the existing line, which changes both the repair method and the fittings needed to tie in a new section correctly.

Codes This Work Follows

Water line work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and International Plumbing Code (IPC) the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners adopts statewide, and typically requires a permit through the local building authority for a full replacement.

The Line Between The Meter And Your House

In Rowlett the water meter sits in a ground box near the curb, and the buried line running from there to your house is typically the homeowner's responsibility, which is worth confirming with the City of Rowlett for your address. Wet patches in a front yard on Dalrock Road or Miller Road with no sprinkler running, and a meter that moves with every tap closed, are the usual signs of a service line leak.

Pressure, Lead And Who Owns Which Part Of The Line

In most cities the water utility owns the line up to the meter and the homeowner owns the line from the meter to the house, but the exact boundary varies. Ask the City of Rowlett utility before assuming. A leak between the meter and the house is generally the homeowner's repair.

Low water pressure has several causes, from a partly closed valve or a failed pressure regulator to mineral buildup in old pipes or a leak in the line itself. A drop that affects the whole house points at the line or regulator, while a drop at one fixture usually does not.

If you suspect an older line is lead, do not guess. Ask your water utility and have the pipe material identified before planning a replacement.

Common Questions

Is emergency water line repair available in Rowlett?+

Yes. A broken supply line is an urgent call and the line is answered 24/7.

If the break is between the meter and the house it is usually the homeowner's repair, and we can explain the options on site. Ask the city utility if you are unsure where its responsibility ends.

Why does my whole house have no water when only the line outside is the problem?+

Because every fixture in the home draws from that single service line before branching inside, a complete failure of that one line, rather than a fixture-level problem, cuts off water to the entire house at once. That pattern, not a slow leak at one sink, is the tell that points outside rather than inside.

Will you need to dig up my whole yard?+

Not necessarily. Trenchless methods handle many water line replacements with only two small access pits rather than an open trench the full length of the line, which we assess and recommend based on the line's actual condition and path.

Is a water line problem covered by homeowner's insurance?+

It depends on the cause and the policy, sudden damage like a line crushed during nearby construction is more likely covered than gradual corrosion on an old line, which most policies treat as normal wear. Check your specific policy language, and we can document the cause to support a claim either way.

How long will I be without water during the repair?+

Most spot repairs and trenchless replacements restore water the same day, often within a few hours once the work itself starts. We give a realistic window before starting so you can plan around it rather than being caught off guard.

Why did the water pressure in my house drop?+

A whole-house drop usually means a failing pressure regulator, a partly closed main valve or a leak in the supply line. A drop at one faucet is usually that fixture's aerator or valve.
