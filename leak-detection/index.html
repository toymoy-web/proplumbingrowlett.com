Home / Services / Leak Detection

Most homeowners who call about a hidden leak do not use the term 'leak detection' at first. They describe a water bill that will not explain itself, a hissing sound behind a wall, or a meter that keeps spinning with every fixture in the house turned off. That is exactly what leak detection covers, and Rowlett Pro Plumbing traces these leaks electronically across Rowlett before anyone opens a wall, floor, or slab.

When A Suspected Leak Cannot Wait Until Morning

Most leak calls can wait for a scheduled visit, but a few cannot. Water coming through a ceiling, a meter that spins fast with every fixture off, or a wet spot spreading across the yard or garage all mean water is leaving the pipes right now.

Our 24/7 line is for those cases. We send a technician with acoustic and thermal equipment to find the source, and you hear the price before any wall or floor is opened.

Before We Arrive

1

Shut Off The Main Valve

Turn the main shutoff at the house clockwise until it stops. Do this first, before you try to find where the water is coming from.

2

Read The Meter

With every fixture and appliance off, check whether the meter dial or the small leak indicator is still moving. That tells the technician if the leak is on your side of the meter.

3

Protect What Is Dry

Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the wet area. Stay clear of outlets and fixtures that water is running through.

4

Call The 24/7 Line

Tell the dispatcher where the water shows up and what the meter is doing. Call (972) 903-8224 and we will give you an arrival window.

Any of those is a leak detection call, whether the leak turns out to be a slab leak, a slow drip inside a wall, or a worn fitting under a fixture.

  • ✓At an outdoor spigot, irrigation line, or the service line between the meter and the house.

How We Find It

Electronic acoustic equipment picks up the specific sound of water escaping a pressurized line, and a correlator cross-references two listening points to narrow the location to a small area before any material is removed. For a suspected slab leak specifically, this is the same method covered in more depth on our slab leak page.

A meter test is the fastest first check. With every fixture off, a moving leak-indicator dial confirms real water loss somewhere in the system, which tells us where to start listening even before a technician arrives on site.

Commercial And Rental Properties

A commercial building or a rental property adds a wrinkle a single-family home does not: the person reporting the problem is often not the one who will approve the repair. We are used to working directly with a property manager or a landlord's own maintenance line, sending photos and a clear explanation of the leak's location so approval does not stall the repair, and we can coordinate around tenant schedules where that matters.

What A Camera Inspection Adds

For a leak inside a wall or under a slab, acoustic tracing usually narrows the location closely enough on its own.

For a suspected leak along a longer service line, or when acoustic results are inconclusive because of ambient noise or a busy street nearby, a sewer or water line camera can confirm the exact condition of the pipe visually rather than relying on sound alone.

We only add a camera inspection when it genuinely changes the diagnosis, not as a standard upsell on every call.

Codes This Work Follows

Leak detection and any resulting repair follow the same Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and International Plumbing Code (IPC) that the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners adopts statewide, the standard every licensed Texas plumber is tested against.

Finding Hidden Leaks From Downtown To The Lakefront

A hidden leak looks different depending on the street. In older homes near Downtown Rowlett it is often a corroded line inside a wall, while in lakeside neighborhoods like Lake Park and Lake Shore Village it can be a line under the slab or an irrigation connection in the yard. The meter test works the same everywhere, so check your meter with every fixture off and call if the dial keeps moving.

What Rowlett Callers Ask Before They Book

Not every leak is inside the house. Irrigation zones, a hot tub or pool line, and the run of pipe between the meter and the house all leak out of sight, and the clues are outdoors. Look for a soft or unusually green patch, a sprinkler zone that runs fast or weak, or a spa that keeps needing water.

Other calls come from owners with more than one unit. A duplex, triplex or small rental can leak inside a wall shared by two units, so the first job is narrowing it to a unit, a floor or a stack before anything is opened. The same applies to a house about to go on the market, where an inspection flagged moisture and the seller needs the source found.

A smaller group calls because another plumber could not find the leak. A second look with different equipment and a different method often finds what a single approach missed, and we tell you plainly if the leak turns out to be outside the plumbing.

Common Questions

Is emergency leak detection available in Rowlett at night?+

Yes. Our line is answered 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Active leaks such as water through a ceiling or a fast-moving meter are treated as urgent calls.

The diagnostic price is confirmed before we start, and the technician explains what the equipment is showing before anything is opened.

My water bill went up but I don't see any water. What should I do?+

Check the water meter with every fixture and appliance off. If the small leak-indicator dial is still moving, water is escaping somewhere in the system, and that is worth a leak detection call rather than waiting for it to show itself.

How is this different from slab leak detection specifically?+

General leak detection covers leaks anywhere in the plumbing system, walls, fixtures, service lines, and slabs. Slab leak detection uses the same acoustic method but is specifically for leaks under a concrete foundation, where the repair path (spot repair, reroute, or repipe) is different from a wall or fixture leak.

Can a leak be found without cutting into a wall at all?+

In most cases yes. Acoustic equipment can pinpoint a leak's location closely enough that any opening made afterward is small and targeted rather than exploratory. A wider opening is only needed on the rare call where the sound is too faint to narrow further, and even then we explain that before doing it, not after.

Can you find a leak in an irrigation line or near a hot tub?+

Yes. We trace pressurized lines outdoors the same way we trace them inside, by isolating sections and listening with sensitive equipment. Tell the dispatcher which zone runs strangely or where the ground stays wet.

Can you check a duplex or small rental building for a leak?+

Yes. We narrow the leak to a unit, a floor or a wall before opening anything, and we explain the findings in writing if a property manager or buyer needs them.

Not sure what you are hearing? Describe it and we will tell you what to check next.
