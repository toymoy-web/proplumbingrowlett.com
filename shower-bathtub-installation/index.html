Shower & Bathtub Installation in Rowlett

A shower or bathtub installation is part carpentry and part plumbing, but the plumbing side, the drain, the supply lines, and the valve, is what actually determines whether the new fixture works properly for years or leaks within a season. Rowlett Pro Plumbing handles the plumbing work for shower and bathtub installations and conversions across Rowlett.

  • ✓Tub-to-shower conversion, a common upgrade for accessibility or simply more usable space.

Why The Plumbing Side Matters Most

The visible tile and fixtures get most of the attention in a bathroom remodel, but a leak behind the wall or under the pan is what causes real, expensive damage months or years later. Correct drain slope, a properly sealed shower pan or tub connection, and a pressure-tested valve before any tile goes up are what actually determine whether the finished project holds up, not just how it looks on installation day.

A tub-to-shower conversion specifically often needs to relocate or modify the drain and supply connections, since a shower pan drains differently than a tub, which is exactly the kind of detail that gets missed when the plumbing work is treated as an afterthought to the carpentry and tile.

Valve Types Worth Knowing About

Valve TypeWhat It Does
Pressure-balanced valveMaintains consistent temperature if another fixture draws water elsewhere in the house
Thermostatic valveAllows precise temperature presetting, common in higher-end installs
Standard compression valveBasic, older-style valve found in many original installations

Anti-Scald Protection Is Not Optional

A pressure-balanced or thermostatic valve is required by current plumbing code specifically because an old-style single-handle valve can let scalding hot water through if someone flushes a toilet or runs a washing machine while a person is in the shower. Replacing an outdated valve during a shower or tub project is a genuine safety upgrade, not just a code checkbox, especially in a household with young children or elderly family members.

Before Your Next Shower Or Tub Project

  • ✓Ask whether your current valve is pressure-balanced or thermostatic, or an older compression type.
  • ✓Have your water heater's thermostat checked, since a setting above 120°F raises scald risk regardless of valve type.
  • ✓Mention any household member with reduced mobility or sensation, since that changes what a safe setup looks like.
  • ✓Confirm the new valve's anti-scald limit is set correctly before the final test run, not just installed.

Signs An Existing Shower Or Tub Needs Attention First

  • ✓Water pooling or draining slowly, a sign the pan or drain slope may already be compromised.
  • ✓Soft or spongy flooring near the base, often a sign of a slow leak that has been going on for a while.
  • ✓Visible cracking or staining around the tub or shower base.
  • ✓A valve that is difficult to adjust to a consistent temperature.

Codes This Work Follows

Shower and bathtub plumbing follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and International Plumbing Code (IPC) the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners adopts statewide, including anti-scald valve requirements and correct drain slope.

Common Questions

Do you do the tile and carpentry too, or just the plumbing?+

We handle the plumbing side, drain, supply lines, and valve, and pressure-test everything thoroughly before the finish work goes on. For full remodels we coordinate timing with your tile or general contractor so the plumbing is ready exactly when they need it, without holding up the rest of the project schedule.

Is a tub-to-shower conversion a big plumbing job?+

It depends on the existing layout. If the drain location works for a shower pan as-is, it is a moderate job. If the drain needs to move, or the valve needs upgrading to a modern pressure-balanced type, it takes noticeably longer, and we confirm which situation you have before quoting.

Can you match a new shower valve to my existing tile and fixtures?+

For most standard finishes, yes. If we are replacing just the valve and trim without a full remodel, we will confirm the exact model and finish match ahead of time before ordering parts so the new hardware looks intentional next to existing tile and fixtures rather than mismatched or out of place.
