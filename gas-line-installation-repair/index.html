Gas Line Installation & Repair in Rowlett

Gas line work is not a place to guess or attempt yourself, a mistake here is a genuine safety hazard, not just an inconvenience. Rowlett Pro Plumbing installs and repairs gas lines across Rowlett for water heaters, ranges, dryers, and outdoor fire features, always to code.

Common Reasons We Get Called

Most gas line calls in Rowlett come from a kitchen remodel adding a gas range, a new outdoor kitchen or fire pit, or a tankless water heater conversion that needs a larger gas supply than the existing tank unit used. A genuine suspected leak is a smaller share of calls but always the highest priority the moment it comes in.

If You Smell Gas, Do This First

Gas Smell Safety

  • ✓Leave the house immediately, do not search for the source yourself.
  • ✓Do not operate light switches, appliances, or your phone inside the house.
  • ✓Once outside and at a safe distance, call your gas utility's emergency line.
  • ✓Do not return inside until the gas utility or a licensed technician confirms it is safe.

What We Handle

  • ✓New gas line runs for a range, dryer, water heater, or outdoor fire feature.
  • ✓Gas line repair for a suspected leak, confirmed by proper leak-detection equipment, never by smell alone.
  • ✓Gas line sizing verification when adding a new appliance, including tankless water heaters.
  • ✓Reconnecting or capping a gas line during a remodel or appliance swap.

Why This Is Licensed-Only Work

Gas line work is regulated for a real reason, an undersized line, a bad connection, or a poorly sealed joint is a genuine fire and carbon monoxide risk, not just a code technicality. A licensed technician pressure-tests every new or repaired gas line before it goes into service, confirming there is no leak under real operating pressure, not just a visual check.

Adding a new gas appliance to an existing line also requires confirming the line and meter can actually supply enough gas for the combined load of everything connected to it. That is exactly the kind of check that gets skipped in an unlicensed install and causes problems later.

Permits For Gas Work

New gas line runs and most repairs in Rowlett require a permit and inspection through the local building department, and we handle that as part of the job rather than leaving it for you to arrange. An uninspected gas line is a real liability at resale, since a buyer's inspector will flag unpermitted gas work every time.

Carbon Monoxide Safety Beyond The Gas Line

A correctly installed and pressure-tested gas line is only half of the safety picture. Every gas appliance, especially a water heater or furnace, needs to vent combustion byproducts outside the home properly, and a blocked or degraded vent can let carbon monoxide build up indoors even with a perfectly sound gas line. A working carbon monoxide detector on every level of the home is a genuinely important, inexpensive safeguard alongside professional gas work.

Codes This Work Follows

Gas line work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and International Plumbing Code (IPC) the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners adopts statewide, including required pressure testing before a new or repaired line is put into service.

Common Questions

Can I run my own gas line for a new range or dryer?+

This is licensed work in Texas, and for good reason, an incorrectly sized or sealed gas line is a real safety hazard. Beyond the safety issue, unlicensed gas work can also create real problems with insurance and with a future home sale.

How do I know if my gas line can support a new appliance?+

The line and meter both need to be checked against the total gas load of everything already connected plus the new appliance. This is a standard part of any quote for adding a gas appliance, not an extra step, since guessing wrong means poor performance or a real safety issue.

How often should gas lines be inspected?+

There is no fixed inspection schedule the way there is for a water heater anniversary. Any time a new gas appliance is added, a remodel disturbs an existing line, or you notice a hissing sound or the distinctive rotten-egg smell added to natural gas for detection, that is the time to have it checked immediately rather than waiting. When in doubt, a quick call costs nothing and settles the question.
