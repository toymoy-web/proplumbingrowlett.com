Drain Cleaning & Unclogging in Rowlett

A slow kitchen sink or a bathtub that drains a little slower every week rarely fixes itself, and by the time it stops draining altogether it is usually an emergency call instead of a routine one. Rowlett Pro Plumbing clears drain clogs across Rowlett, from a single slow fixture to a full main line backup, and diagnoses which one you actually have before quoting a price.

Common Drain Problems We Clear

  • ✓Kitchen sink clog: grease and food buildup, the most common single-fixture clog.
  • ✓Bathroom drain clog: hair and soap buildup in tub, shower, and bathroom sink drains.
  • ✓Main line backup: multiple fixtures backing up at once, usually a sewer line issue.
  • ✓Slow drain: not fully clogged yet, but noticeably slower than it used to be.

Not sure if it is one fixture or the main line? Tell us what is backing up and we will tell you.

Signs You Are Looking At A Main Line, Not A Single Fixture

  • ✓More than one drain is slow or backed up at the same time, not just one sink or tub.
  • ✓The lowest drain in the house, usually a basement floor drain or the lowest-level shower, backs up first.
  • ✓Water or sewage comes up in one fixture when you run a completely different one, like the toilet backing up when you run the washing machine.
  • ✓A recent tree removal or known mature tree near the sewer line's path adds real risk of root intrusion.

How We Clear It

A single slow fixture is usually cleared with a drain snake or cable run directly through that fixture's line, and it is typically a same-visit fix. A main line backup, where more than one fixture is affected at the same time, points to a clog further down the shared sewer line. That usually calls for hydro jetting or a heavier-duty cable to fully clear rather than re-clog within a few weeks.

Root intrusion is a real factor in older parts of Rowlett with mature trees near the sewer line, and a camera inspection can confirm whether roots are the actual cause before recommending a repeat-clog fix like hydro jetting over a one-time snake.

What Not To Pour Down A Drain

Cooking grease, coffee grounds, and eggshells are the most common causes of a repeat kitchen clog in this area, and none of them break down the way a garbage disposal makes them seem to. Pouring grease down a drain while it is liquid still lets it cool and solidify further down the pipe, often at a bend where it then catches everything that follows it.

When To Stop DIY And Call

A store-bought chemical drain cleaner is fine for a genuinely minor clog, but repeated use on the same drain is a real warning sign, not a fix. Chemical cleaners do not clear a solid blockage further down the line, and pouring one down a drain right before a technician arrives can also be a real safety hazard once a cable or camera goes into that same line.

If a plunger and a single round of a store cleaner have not cleared it, or if the clog keeps coming back within a few weeks, that is the point to call rather than keep trying the same fix. A repeat clog on the same fixture almost always means the actual blockage is further down the line than a plunger or a bottle of cleaner can reach.

Chemical Drain Cleaner Safety

Before Using A Chemical Cleaner

  • ✓Never mix two different drain cleaner products, or a cleaner with bleach or ammonia, the combination can create dangerous fumes.
  • ✓Ventilate the room and avoid leaning directly over the drain while it works.
  • ✓Do not use a plunger right after pouring a chemical cleaner, it can splash the chemical back at you.
  • ✓If a cleaner has already been used and the clog is still there, tell the technician before they start, since cabling or camera work in a chemical-treated line needs extra care.

Codes This Work Follows

Drain and sewer work in Texas falls under the same statewide plumbing code framework as every other service on this site, the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and International Plumbing Code (IPC) adopted by the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners. A repair is done to a real enforceable standard, not just to whatever clears the clog fastest.

Common Questions

Why does my kitchen sink keep clogging even after I clear it?+

A recurring kitchen clog is almost always grease and food particles rebuilding on the same section of pipe wall a snake did not fully clear. Hydro jetting scours the full pipe diameter rather than punching a single hole through the clog, which is why it holds up longer on a fixture that clogs repeatedly.

How do I know if it's one drain or the main line?+

If only one fixture is slow or backed up, it is almost always that fixture's own line. If two or more fixtures back up around the same time, especially the lowest drain in the house, that points to the shared main line rather than an individual fixture.
